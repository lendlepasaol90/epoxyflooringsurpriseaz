The basics

How a chip floor is built.

After grinding and repairs, we roll a pigmented base coat across the slab and broadcast vinyl flakes into it by hand while it’s still wet. On a full-broadcast floor we keep going until no base color shows through. Once the base cures, the excess chips are scraped and vacuumed, leaving a firmly embedded layer.

Then the clear goes on. One or two coats of polyaspartic or polyurethane seal the chips, set the final texture and provide the wear surface. More clear means a smoother finish; less clear leaves more of the chip texture for grip.

Why choose it

Why chip floors are so popular.

Flake systems combine durability, looks and forgiveness better than almost any other finish.

Dust disappears

The busy pattern camouflages dirt, tire marks and small debris between sweeps.

Built-in texture

Chips add a slight grip underfoot that solid colors don’t have.

Extra thickness

The chip layer adds build and helps the floor resist impacts and abrasion.

Hides slab flaws

Patched cracks and uneven color in the concrete disappear under the broadcast.

Options

Choosing chip size and coverage.

Use the blend preview on our homepage to get a feel for colors, then confirm with real samples at your measure.

Most common

Full broadcast, 1/4" chip

Complete chip coverage with medium-size flakes is the standard for garages. It’s the most forgiving with dust and the most uniform in texture.

Alternatives

Partial broadcast or micro chip

A light scatter of chips over a solid base looks more minimal and suits interiors. Tiny 1/16" chips create a fine, granite-like texture that reads clean in showrooms and casitas.

Surprise notes

Blends that suit the desert palette.

Surprise homes lean toward stucco in sand, taupe and gray, with desert landscaping and stone accents. Chip blends that echo those tones tie the garage or patio to the rest of the house. Tans and browns hide the local dust well; grays and charcoals look sharp next to modern exteriors.

Outdoors and near open garage doors, the clear coat is what keeps the colors true. We use a UV-stable clear so blends don’t shift toward yellow after a few summers.

Install days

From bare slab to sealed chip floor.

A typical garage chip floor takes one to two days, depending on size and the clear-coat chemistry.

  1. Grind & repairThe slab is ground, cracks are filled and the surface is vacuumed clean.
  2. Base coatA color-matched base goes down in sections so it stays wet for the broadcast.
  3. BroadcastChips are thrown by hand to full coverage across each section.
  4. Scrape & vacuumAfter the base cures, loose and standing chips are scraped flat and removed.
  5. Clear coatThe topcoat seals everything in and sets the final texture.

Care

Low-maintenance by design.

Sweep weekly with a soft broom and mop with a neutral cleaner as needed. The texture means a little more effort with a mop than a smooth floor, but the pattern means it rarely looks dirty in the first place.

Avoid dragging metal furniture or equipment across the floor, and use pads under sharp loads.

Questions

Decorative Flake Flooring: quick answers.

How many colors can a blend have?

Most standard blends have three to six colors. Custom blends are possible on larger jobs.

Will the chips come loose?

No. They’re embedded in the base and sealed under the clear coat, so they can’t pop out.

Can I use flake on a patio?

Yes, with a UV-stable clear and a bit more texture for wet feet. See our patio page for details.

Is flake harder to clean than a smooth floor?

Slightly, because of the texture, but it hides dirt so well that most owners clean it less often.

Do you have a sample board?

Yes. We bring physical chip samples to every measure so you can see them in your own light.

Can you do a logo or border with chips?

Simple borders and color zones are possible. Logos are better done as a separate decal or paint under the clear.
