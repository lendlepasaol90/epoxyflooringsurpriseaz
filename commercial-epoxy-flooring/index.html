The basics

A floor specified for how you work.

Commercial systems start with the same fundamentals as any floor we coat: diamond grinding, crack and joint repair, and a properly bonded base. What changes is the build. An auto bay gets a thick, chemical-resistant system that can handle brake fluid, hot tires and dropped tools. A salon gets a topcoat that stands up to color, bleach and constant mopping. A gym gets impact resistance and a sheen that hides scuffs.

Before we quote, we ask about foot and cart traffic, rolling equipment, the chemicals you use, how and how often you clean, and any requirements from your landlord, insurer or industry. All of it goes into a written scope, so the floor you get is built for the way your business actually runs.

Why choose it

What businesses get from it.

For many commercial spaces, a coated floor is one of the highest-impact upgrades per dollar.

A sharper first impression

Bright, clean floors make a showroom, lobby or service bay look well run.

Chemical resistance

Built to handle oils, solvents, salon chemicals and strong cleaners without staining.

Faster cleaning

Seamless surfaces with no grout lines mean quicker mopping and scrubbing at close.

Lower lifetime cost

Less patching, replacing and downtime than tile, VCT or bare sealed concrete.

Surprise notes

Surprise businesses, Surprise schedules.

Surprise has a wide mix of businesses along Bell Road, Grand Avenue and the Loop 303 corridor: auto repair and detailing shops, fitness studios, medical and dental offices, salons, retail and light industrial units. Very few of them can close for a week. We phase work room by room, coat over nights or weekends, and choose faster-curing products when reopening time is tight.

Many local businesses also see a busy season when winter visitors arrive and spring training fills the city. If you can plan ahead, scheduling your floor during the slower summer months means it’s finished and fully cured before your busiest stretch.

Install days

How a commercial job runs.

Timelines depend on square footage, the system and how we phase it. Smaller suites can often be finished over a single weekend.

  1. Site reviewWe walk the space, review traffic, equipment and chemicals, and agree on a schedule.
  2. PrepGrind, repair cracks and joints, and test the slab for moisture.
  3. BuildBase, body and topcoats go down to spec, with cure windows between them.
  4. Lines & zonesColor zones, walkway borders, bay lines or logos are added as planned.
  5. Hand-offA walk-through with your team and a written cure schedule for reopening.

Care

Keeping a commercial floor sharp.

Dust-mop or sweep daily to remove grit, and clean with a neutral or manufacturer-approved cleaner. On larger floors, an auto-scrubber with soft pads works well. Avoid aggressive degreasers unless your topcoat is rated for them.

Walk-off mats at every entry catch most of the dirt before it spreads. We can suggest a recoat schedule for high-traffic lanes so the floor never wears through to the color coat.

Questions

Commercial Epoxy Flooring: quick answers.

Can you work after hours?

Yes. Nights and weekends are common on commercial jobs, and we can phase larger spaces so part of the business stays open.

Can you add our logo or brand colors?

Yes. Color zones can be built into the system, and logos can be applied under the clear coat so they don’t wear off.

Is the floor slip-resistant?

We add texture where it’s needed, especially at entries, wash areas and anywhere the floor gets wet.

Will it meet our health or industry requirements?

Seamless coated floors are common in clinics, labs and food-prep areas. Tell us your requirements and we’ll specify a system to match.

How long will we be closed?

It depends on the size and the system. Fast-cure products and phased work can keep downtime to a day or a weekend.

Can you coat over our existing floor?

We remove tile, VCT and failing coatings. A well-bonded existing coating can sometimes be prepped and recoated after testing.
