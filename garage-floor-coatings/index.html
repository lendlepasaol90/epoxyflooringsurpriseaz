The basics

Three layers, three jobs.

A coated garage floor is a system, not a can of paint. The base coat penetrates the ground concrete and bonds to it. The middle layer carries the color, either a solid pigment or a full broadcast of vinyl chips pressed into the wet base. The topcoat is the clear armor that takes tire scuffs, dropped tools and spilled fluids.

For most Surprise garages we build on an epoxy or polyurea base and finish with a polyaspartic clear. The base is chosen for bond strength and how much the slab is likely to move; the clear is chosen because it stays glossy and doesn’t amber where sunlight reaches the floor. Your quote spells out each layer so you know exactly what’s going down.

Why choose it

What a coated garage changes.

The first sweep usually sells people on it. Dust that used to cling to raw concrete lifts away, and the room looks twice as bright.

No more hot-tire peel

A fully bonded, thick system doesn’t soften and lift when you park after a summer drive.

Spills wipe up

Oil, coolant and pool chemicals bead up on the clear and wipe away instead of leaving a permanent shadow.

A brighter room

Light chip blends and a gloss clear reflect light into garages with few windows.

Cleaner storage

Shelving, freezers and bins sit on a sealed floor that doesn’t shed concrete dust onto everything.

Options

Pick a finish that fits the garage.

Most Surprise homeowners land on one of two directions. We bring samples of both to the measure.

Most popular

Full-broadcast flake

Vinyl chips cover the base completely, then get scraped and sealed. The pattern hides monsoon dust, tire marks and small imperfections, and adds a light texture underfoot. Blends run from warm desert tones to cool grays.

Clean & modern

Solid color or metallic

A single solid color with a clear coat gives a crisp, shop-floor look that suits workbenches and car projects. Metallic epoxy delivers a marbled showroom finish for collector garages; it’s best where direct sun is limited or under a UV-stable clear.

Surprise notes

Golf carts, RVs and 115-degree afternoons.

Garages across Surprise see a mix of use you don’t find everywhere. Active-adult communities like Sun City Grand have golf carts charging in the bay every night. Newer subdivisions toward Loop 303 often have tandem or RV-height garages with long slabs and big doors. Many homes were finished with builder-grade concrete that was never sealed.

Summer heat is the big variable. A garage slab can get hot enough in July and August to shorten the working time of fast-cure coatings, so we start at first light, mix in smaller batches and keep the door closed when we can. The first few feet inside a west-facing door get the most sun, so the clear coat there matters most.

Install days

How a garage job runs.

Clear out the garage the day before, except for anything bolted to the wall. Most two- and three-car garages are finished in one to two working days.

  1. Grind & vacuumDiamond grinders with attached vacuums open the surface. Edges are hand-ground along the walls and stem walls.
  2. RepairCracks are chased open and filled, pits and spalls are patched, and everything is ground flush.
  3. Base & broadcastThe base coat goes down and, for a flake floor, chips are broadcast by hand until the base disappears.
  4. Scrape & sealLoose chips are scraped and vacuumed, then the clear topcoat is rolled on and back-rolled for an even film.
  5. Cure cardWe walk the floor with you and leave the dates for walking, storage and parking.

Pricing

Garage pricing, explained.

We quote after measuring because two garages the same size can need very different amounts of work. These are the factors that move the number most.

Square footageA three-car or RV garage simply needs more material and time than a two-car.
Slab conditionOld paint, sealer or a failed kit coating has to be ground off first.
Crack repairWide, long or spalled cracks need more filler and grinding time.
System & finishA chip system with a UV-stable clear is a bigger investment up front than one solid coat, and it outlasts it by years.

Care

Living with the new floor.

Sweep or dust-mop once a week, especially after a dust storm, so grit doesn’t scratch the clear. Mop with warm water and a pH-neutral cleaner when needed. Slip a puck or a square of plywood under kickstands and jack stands, and keep a mat at the house door.

Wait for the date on your cure card before parking. From then on it’s ready for the daily commute, the cart rolling in and out every afternoon, and the odd dropped wrench.

Questions

Garage Floor Coatings: quick answers.

Do I need to empty the whole garage?

Yes, anything that isn’t fastened down. Wall cabinets, water softeners and wall-mounted chargers can stay; we mask them and coat neatly around them.

Can the golf cart go back in the same day?

Usually not. Even fast-cure systems need time before wheel traffic. Your cure card gives the date based on the products and the temperature that day.

Will hot tires lift the coating?

Not with a properly bonded professional system. Hot-tire pickup is a problem with thin kits over etched concrete, not with a ground, high-build floor.

What about the expansion joints?

We can fill and coat over them for a seamless look, or keep them open where the slab needs room to move. We’ll recommend one after looking at your slab.

Is a flake floor slippery when wet?

The chips give a mild texture. For extra grip near the door, we can add a fine anti-slip aggregate to the clear coat.

Will the color fade near the door?

Not with a UV-stable polyaspartic clear. That’s the main reason we use it as the top layer in Surprise garages.
