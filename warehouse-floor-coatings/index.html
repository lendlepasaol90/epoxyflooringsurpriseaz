The basics

Industrial floors, built for wheels.

An industrial coating is a thicker, harder build than anything we put in a home. We typically specify a high-build epoxy, sometimes with a self-leveling body coat, topped with an abrasion-resistant urethane or polyaspartic. That combination resists the grinding wear of hard forklift wheels and the chemical exposure common in fabrication and maintenance areas.

Joints get special attention. The edges of saw-cut control joints chip and spall under steel and polyurethane wheels, and the damage spreads. We clean out and fill joints with a semi-rigid filler that supports the edges, then coat the floor so it reads as one continuous surface. Safety lines, pedestrian walkways, staging zones and hazard markings are painted to your facility plan.

Why choose it

Why coat a warehouse floor.

A coated floor pays for itself through less dust, less damage and easier operations.

An end to concrete dust

Sealed concrete stops the dusting that settles on inventory, racking and equipment.

Brighter, safer aisles

Light gray coatings reflect overhead light and make spills and debris easier to spot.

Protected joints

Filled joints keep forklift wheels from chipping and spalling slab edges.

Clear traffic control

Painted lanes, walkways and zones guide forklifts and keep pedestrians separated.

Options

Two common industrial builds.

We match the build thickness and topcoat to your traffic and the work done on the floor.

Standard traffic

High-build epoxy with a urethane top

The right fit for storage, light manufacturing and moderate forklift use. Good chemical resistance and a hard, cleanable surface.

Heavy traffic

Self-leveling body coat with an abrasion-resistant top

A thicker system for high-volume distribution, heavy equipment and constant turning traffic, where wear lanes would quickly break through a thinner build.

Surprise notes

Industrial growth around Loop 303.

The Loop 303 corridor and the land around it have become one of the Phoenix area’s busiest stretches for new distribution, logistics and light-industrial buildings, and Surprise sits right along it. New tilt-up buildings often come with slabs that still carry curing compounds or sealers from construction, which have to be ground off before any coating will bond.

Older buildings bring the opposite problem: worn wheel lanes, spalled joints and oil-soaked areas. In both cases we test the slab for moisture and hardness, then plan the install in phases so you can keep receiving, picking and shipping while we work.

Install days

How we coat a warehouse.

Industrial installs are planned with your operations team. Larger buildings are coated in zones, often over nights and weekends.

  1. AssessmentWe review traffic, equipment, chemicals, layout and the condition of slab and joints.
  2. PrepLarge ride-on grinders or shot blasting remove curing compounds, sealers and contamination.
  3. Joints & repairsJoints are cleaned and filled, and spalls and cracks are repaired.
  4. CoatingPrimer, body coat and topcoat go down to the specified thickness.
  5. StripingAisle lines, walkways, zones and hazard markings are painted to your plan.

Care

Maintaining an industrial floor.

Sweep or auto-scrub regularly to remove the grit that acts like sandpaper under wheels. Use cleaners compatible with your topcoat and soft or medium pads, not aggressive stripping pads.

Check main forklift lanes and turning areas a few times a year. Recoating the topcoat in high-wear lanes before it wears through extends the life of the whole system for a fraction of the cost of a full redo.

Questions

Warehouse Floor Coatings: quick answers.

Can you work while we stay open?

Yes. We phase the floor into zones and schedule work around shifts, dock times and weekends.

Do you paint safety lines?

Yes. We stripe aisles, pedestrian walkways, staging zones and hazard areas to your facility layout.

How long does an industrial coating last?

It depends on traffic, wheel type and maintenance. Recoating high-wear lanes before they wear through extends the life of the system.

Can you repair damaged joints?

Yes. Joint cleaning, filling and edge repair are part of our industrial systems.

Can it be slip-resistant?

Yes. Texture can be added to the topcoat in wet areas, ramps and walkways.

What if the slab has moisture issues?

We test before quoting. If vapor is high, we use a moisture-mitigating primer under the system.
