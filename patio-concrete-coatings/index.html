The basics

Coatings made for life outdoors.

Outdoor coatings have to do two things an interior floor never deals with: hold up to relentless UV, and stay safe when they’re wet. That rules out standard epoxy, which yellows and chalks in the sun. We use polyaspartic and aliphatic systems that keep their color outside, and we build texture into the surface with chips or fine aggregate so it grips underfoot.

Prep doesn’t change just because the slab is outside. We pressure-wash, grind off old sealers and paint, chase and fill cracks, and patch spalled or pitted areas. On exterior concrete we also check for efflorescence, slope and drainage, because water that ponds on a coated surface will find any weak spot.

Options

Choosing an outdoor finish.

Color matters more outdoors than inside. Dark surfaces in Surprise sun get hot enough to be uncomfortable, so we steer most clients toward lighter tones.

Most popular

Light flake with a textured clear

A light, sandy chip blend hides dirt and leaf litter, adds natural grip, and ties in with stucco and desert landscaping. Our most-requested patio and entry finish.

Minimal

Solid light color with grit

One light color with anti-slip aggregate in the clear gives a clean, modern look around pools and outdoor kitchens while staying cooler underfoot.

Surprise notes

Sun, heat and hard water.

By early afternoon in July, an uncoated patio facing south or west can be too hot to stand on barefoot, and dark coatings make it worse. We recommend light colors for anywhere people walk without shoes, and we install in the morning when the slab is coolest so the products level and bond properly.

Hard water is the other local headache. Sprinkler overspray and pool splash leave white mineral deposits, and moisture wicking through older slabs can push efflorescence to the surface. We remove existing deposits during prep, and a sealed, coated surface makes new ones much easier to wipe off.

What we plan around

At every outdoor measure we check slope, drainage, sprinkler overspray, sun exposure and any staining, then plan the system around them.

Install days

Coating an outdoor slab.

Most patios, entries and walkways are done in one to two days. During monsoon season we watch the forecast closely and reschedule if storms threaten a fresh coat.

  1. Clean & grindPressure-wash, then grind off old sealers, paint and surface contamination.
  2. RepairFill cracks, patch spalls and correct small low spots where water collects.
  3. Base & textureLay the base coat and broadcast chips or aggregate for grip.
  4. UV-stable clearSeal everything with a clear rated for constant sun.

Care

Outdoor upkeep.

Hose it off and sweep regularly, especially after dust storms. For grime, use a neutral cleaner and a soft deck brush. If you pressure-wash, use a wide fan tip and keep the wand at a distance.

Lift planters now and then so water doesn’t sit under them, and clean grill drips before they bake on. Adjust sprinkler heads that spray the slab to cut down on mineral spotting.

Questions

Patio & Concrete Coatings: quick answers.

Will the coating get hot in summer?

Any outdoor surface gets warm in Arizona sun. Pale tones run much cooler to the touch than charcoal or brown, so we point most patio and pool-deck clients toward them.

Can you coat around a pool?

Yes. We use textured, chemical-resistant systems and work carefully around coping, drains and skimmers.

Is it slippery when wet?

Not with the right texture. We add chips or aggregate for grip, and more of it around pools and steps.

Can you coat pavers?

We focus on poured concrete. Pavers usually call for a penetrating sealer rather than a coating.

What about cracks in my patio?

We fill and repair them. Active cracks can reappear over time, and we’ll point out which ones look likely to.

How soon can I use the patio?

Foot traffic is often fine the next day. Furniture and grills usually go back a day or two later.
